# Voice

**How you actually write.** Read this before writing anything that goes out
under your name: essays, applications, posts, bios, cover letters, texts you
will paste and send.

This file starts empty on purpose. It gets filled by sampling your real writing,
not by guessing. Ask Claude to build it and it will follow the protocol in
`~/.claude/rules/writing.md` under "Writing as the user."

---

## How to fill this in

Point Claude at three or more pieces of your own unedited writing. Old
application essays work well, especially middle drafts rather than the final
version someone else edited. Long messages, journal entries, anything you wrote
without an audience. Avoid anything professionally edited: it has been sanded
down by somebody else and it is not you.

Then ask it to measure rather than guess.

## Measurements

Fill these in from actual samples.

- **Contractions vs formal constructions:** ___ to ___
  (Count "don't / it's / I've" against "do not / it is / I have". Most people run
  heavily contracted. Assistant drafts usually invert this, and fixing it is the
  single highest-leverage change available.)
- **Average sentence length:** ___ words
- **Variance:** ___ (high variance is human, uniform length is a machine)

## How you open

Scene, quote, claim, or confession? Quote two real examples.

## How you close

Concrete detail, callback to your opening, or a final crafted line? Note how
often you actually land a perfect closer, because it is probably not every time.

## Structural habits

Things you do repeatedly. Build paragraphs around quotes from real people? Ask
questions you do not answer? Two-beat reversals? Long sentences held together
with commas?

## Where you leave slack

Find the sentence a copy editor would cut from your own writing. That sentence
is usually the most human thing on the page. Note what kind of slack you leave
so it does not get edited out of you.

## What to stop doing

The specific ways drafts written for you come out wrong. Fill this in after the
first time something reads as not-you.

---

## The test

Read the draft out loud. If it sounds like a competent stranger being efficient,
it is wrong.
