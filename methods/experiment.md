# Running an experiment

**Falsifier:** state the result that would kill the hypothesis, BEFORE
looking. Write it down where it cannot be edited afterwards.

**Cheapest test:** what is the smallest n that could still surprise me?

## Questions that change the experiment

- What do I expect to see? Commit to it in writing first. An experiment whose
  outcome was not predicted cannot surprise you, and a result that cannot
  surprise you teaches nothing.
- What is the denominator? A count without one is the shape of a measurement
  rather than a measurement. "4 animations still run" and "4 out of 214" are
  opposite findings.
- What is the control? Measuring the treated group alone measures nothing.
- Could this separate on something other than what I think? Two corpora of
  different FILE TYPES will separate on file type, confidently, and mean
  nothing.
- What n would make me believe it? Say the number before collecting, or every
  n becomes "enough" the moment it agrees.
- Am I about to report the confirmation and bury the null?

## The trap specific to experiments

Pre-registering feels like bureaucracy until the first time a result comes
back against you and you notice how much you want to reinterpret it.
