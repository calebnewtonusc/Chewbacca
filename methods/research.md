# Researching something

**Falsifier:** what would I find that means this whole line of thinking is
already solved, or already known to fail?

**Cheapest test:** search for the thing that would embarrass me most if it
existed, first.

## Questions that change the research

- Who has the strongest reason to have already built this, and what did they
  find? Go read their actual output, not a summary of it.
- What is the number, not the claim? "Distant analogies increase novelty" is
  a vibe; "increase novelty at LOWER efficacy due to surface dissimilarity"
  changes the design.
- Does the evidence conflict? Conflicting sources are more useful than
  agreeing ones, because the conflict is where the real decision lives.
  Record both sides and pick, do not average them.
- Am I searching to learn or to confirm? Rewrite the query as if trying to
  prove myself wrong and run it too.
- What am I citing from memory? Anything unverified is an assumption wearing
  a citation's clothes.
- How many searches is enough, and did I say so before starting? Six searches
  is not a literature review and the writeup should say which it was.

## The trap specific to research

Finding the prior art late means rewriting everything, so the temptation is
to do it after the first draft. That ordering has never once been correct.
