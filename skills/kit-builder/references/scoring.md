# Scoring, with worked examples

The test is only useful if it can say no. These are real scores, including the ones that
failed.

## Passed

**Applications, score 7.** Spans weeks. The bottleneck is that nobody ever asked this
person the right question about their own life. A committee scores it against criteria the
form does not state. Word limits are the one thing a model is genuinely bad at. Rare and
high-stakes. One canonical set of numbers feeds a dozen essays and a resume. And there is
a process that consultants charge thousands for. Shipped as apply-kit.

**Accommodations, score 7.** All seven, and the mechanical one is the sharpest: exam
booking windows are several business days, and missing one means the accommodation does
not exist for that exam regardless of what the letter says. Shipped.

**Estate administration, score 7.** Forty institutions over eighteen months, each with its
own process, while grieving. Property 1 alone almost carries it: nobody in that state can
hold that much in their head.

**Fundraise, score 7.** Property 6 is the killer. Metrics change weekly and the deck does
not, so the number in the deck, the number in the memo, and the number said out loud drift
apart. That is rule 5 with money attached.

**Promotion packet, score 7.** A published rubric, and a year of evidence nobody kept. The
kit is mostly the logging, which is exactly the shape property 1 rewards.

## Failed, and these are the useful ones

**Hard conversation prep, score 3.** Real value, wrong container. One session, no
artifacts, no arithmetic, no evaluator scoring a document. It is a genuinely good skill
and a bad kit. Building a repo around it would add ceremony to something that should
happen in one conversation.

**Launch checklist, score 4.** Spans sessions and has some state, but the bottleneck is
generation and recall, not extraction. Nobody needs to be interviewed about their own
launch. Write the checklist.

**Reading one hard book, score 4.** Spans sessions and there is a real method. But no
hidden evaluator, no mechanical constraint, and nothing has to stay consistent across
artifacts. Skill.

**Meal planning, score 2.** Listed only because somebody always suggests it.

## The pattern in the failures

Every failed idea has property 1 and nothing else. **Spanning multiple sessions is
necessary and nowhere near sufficient.** The question that actually separates them is
property 2: is the hard part getting something out of this person's head, or is the hard
part producing text? If it is producing text, a skill does it.

## The tiebreaker at exactly 5

Ask whether a wrong fact costs anything. If somebody could carry a false number for six
weeks and only find out in a room where it matters, build the kit, because rule 5 and
`stale.sh` are the whole reason kits exist rather than long prompts. If a wrong fact is
just an edit, write a skill.
