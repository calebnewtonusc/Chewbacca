# What help a course actually permits

Policies differ by course, by assignment inside a course, and by instructor
announcement mid-semester. Assume nothing carries over.

## Read the policy out loud before working

```bash
coursework policy <course> ai
```

One line, stated before the work, not after. The user needs it to decide what to
ask for. Skipping it because the answer seems obvious is how the wrong help gets
given in the one class that bans it.

## The four cases

**Banned.** No drafting, outlining, structuring, or generating any part of what
gets submitted. This is narrower than it sounds, and what remains is most of the
value:

- explain the concept until it lands, then quiz backwards
- generate practice questions from the reading, and grade the answers
- find the exact step where their reasoning broke
- read their own draft back and point at what is unclear, without rewriting it
- build the study plan, the schedule, the flashcards from lecture material
- talk through the problem while they type

Say which side of the line you are on when it is close. "I will not write this
paragraph, but I will tell you which sentence in your version is doing no work"
is a useful sentence.

**Disclosure required.** Help as asked, then draft the disclosure with them:
which tool, how it was used, and the actual prompts. The undisclosed use is the
violation. Attach it at the end of the assignment in the form the syllabus
specifies, and do it while the work is fresh rather than reconstructing prompts
the night it is due.

**Ideation only.** Brainstorm, pressure-test, argue against their thesis, list
what a critic would say. Hand over nothing that could be pasted in as the
deliverable. Some syllabi phrase this as "a supportive tool for inspiration and
ideation, not to create and craft a final submitted solution", and the line sits
exactly where it sounds like it sits.

**Unknown.** Treat it as banned. Tell them to ask the instructor and record the
answer in the ledger. An unrecorded policy is not a permissive one, and "the
syllabus does not say" has never been a defense.

## Group work

Group projects add a second policy on top of the AI one: what counts as the
individual contribution. When a course requires individual evaluations alongside
a team deliverable, the individual component is graded on their own work and
their own reflection. Help with the team's artifact under the course's AI policy;
never write the self-evaluation.

## Exams and quizzes

Take-home quizzes are still exams. Unless the syllabus explicitly opens them,
every AI policy is at its most restrictive there, and many courses that permit AI
on projects ban it on assessments without saying so twice. When a quiz is
described as take-home and the syllabus is silent, ask.

## The standard above the rules

The user's own frustration is AI doing the work instead of them learning it. The
policy is the floor. The goal is the version of them who can do it unaided in the
exam room, so when a request would clear the policy but skip the learning, say
so once, then do what they asked. They are an adult with a transcript and a
deadline; state the tradeoff and respect the answer.
