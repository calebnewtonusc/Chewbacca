# Exam run-up and post-mortem

## Start from the exam, not from the notes

Three facts before planning anything: the date, the exact material range, and
the format. All three are usually in the syllabus, and the range is the one
people get wrong. "Chapters 1 to 5" and "everything through week 5" can differ
by a whole chapter.

Then check what the exam is worth and what the makeup policy is. A course with
no makeup exams and one excusable absence changes what a fever on exam morning
costs, and that is worth knowing in advance rather than at 7am.

## The plan

**Two weeks out, or whenever the run starts: diagnostic.** Closed book, across
the whole range, 20 to 30 questions, no studying first. Score it. The result
sorts the material into solid, shaky, and absent, and everything after this is
aimed at the last two categories. Skipping this step is why study time gets spent
on the chapter that was already fine.

**The middle sessions.** Spaced, interleaved, retrieval first. Each one opens by
reasking what was missed last time. New material gets a first pass, then goes
into the same rotation. Keep sessions under 90 minutes; the second hour of a
three-hour session is mostly rereading with the lights on.

**Practice under exam conditions once.** Same time limit, same format, no notes,
no phone. A 60-minute multiple choice exam is a different skill from untimed
recall, and the first time under time pressure should not be the graded one.

**The last session.** Retrieval only. No new chapters, no rereading, nothing
started that cannot be finished. If something is still absent the night before,
it stays absent; spend the time making the solid material automatic.

**Sleep.** Consolidation is not a metaphor here. A night of sleep does more for
retention than the hours it displaces, and this is the trade people take backward
every single term.

## Cramming, when that is the actual situation

Sometimes the exam is tomorrow. Say so plainly instead of prescribing a two-week
plan that no longer exists, then cram well:

- Weight by what the exam weights. The chapter with three lectures beats the one
  with a paragraph.
- Retrieval, not reading, even now.
- Nothing new in the last hour. Consolidate what is already half-there.
- Sleep anyway. Cramming through the night trades a known cost against a small
  gain.

## The post-mortem

Twenty minutes with the returned exam, and it is worth more than the next three
study sessions.

Sort every wrong answer by cause:

1. **Never knew it.** It was not in the notes, deck, or practice set. The intake
   failed. Fix what gets captured from lecture.
2. **Knew it, could not retrieve it.** Recognized the answer instantly when seen.
   Not a knowledge problem. More testing, less reading.
3. **Confused two things.** Build the discrimination pair, drill both directions.
4. **Misread the question.** Process, not content. Slow down on the stem,
   underline the operative word.

Count the four. The distribution is the prescription, and it is frequently not
the one the user expects.

Also check the regrade window before doing any of this. Many courses give one
week from the day the exam was returned, require a specific request format, and
reject anything incomplete without notice. That deadline expires while the
post-mortem is being scheduled.
