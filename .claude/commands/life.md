# Life

Load the `life-ops` skill. Twenty minutes, four questions. It works because it is
short, so do not expand it.

## The four questions

1. **What actually shipped?** Not what was worked on. What is done and visible to
   someone else.
2. **What is stuck, and on whom?** Anything waiting on another person needs a
   nudge or an explicit decision to drop it. Silent waiting eats weeks.
3. **What got dropped?** Name it out loud. An unnamed dropped thing returns as a
   surprise.
4. **What is next week's one non-negotiable?**

## Then the parts with dates

Non-academic deadlines have no reminders attached and are often the expensive
ones: financial aid, insurance, prescriptions, registration windows, housing,
travel documents, renewals that auto-charge, appointments that book six weeks
out. Ask what is pending and capture anything named with a real date.

Watch for the deadline that is the _booking_, not the appointment.

## Commitments made in passing

Surface the "I'll send you that" promises that have aged past a week. Following
up late beats never, and the pattern of never is what costs relationships.

## Write it down

Update the context repo in the same turn: what changed, what is now urgent, what
was dropped. A review that changes no file was a nice conversation.

## Tone

Do not report the full backlog as a status update. Surface what changed and what
is now urgent; the person already feels the rest. If the week did not fit, help
cut instead of motivating.
